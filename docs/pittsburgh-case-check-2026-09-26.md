# Two bounded Pittsburgh cases for the next Track 1 experiment

Research date: 2026-09-26. No outreach. Jack Billings's ["You Can't Build That Here" series](https://www.prohousingpgh.org/ycbth) is an advocacy organization's primary account of its own case arguments. It is useful for finding parcels and hypotheses, but it is not an authoritative statement of current law, an adjudication, or evidence that a proposed deal would pencil out.

## Case 1: Lanark Street, Fineview, affordable homes

**Why it is useful:** a real community land trust project shows how the proposed housing form, neighborhood objective, zoning path, and financing interact. Billings's [2023 account of 21 Lanark St](https://www.prohousingpgh.org/blog/you-cant-build-that-here-21-lanark-st) says this historic rowhouse at parcel 21-C-213 has a narrow 1,800 sq ft lot in a detached housing district and uses it as a comparison for City of Bridges and Fineview Citizens Council's nearby eight home proposal. It recounts variance approval, neighbor challenge, and a 2023 court reversal. Treat the legal narrative and estimated delay/cost there as attributed advocacy reporting, not a verified current entitlement for any Lanark parcel.

**More direct project record:** URA [July 2021 HOF Advisory Board minutes](https://www.ura.org/media/W1siZiIsIjIwMjIvMDEvMDYvN2l1eHN0OGxnZF83LjEuMjFfSE9GX0Fkdmlzb3J5X0JvYXJkX01pbnV0ZXMucGRmIl1d/7.1.21_HOF%20Advisory%20Board%20Minutes.pdf) describe City of Bridges seeking predevelopment funding for five new and three renovated affordable for-sale homes on Lanark Street, with a recommended $100,000 FSDP predevelopment loan for acquisition and predevelopment. URA [November 2023 board minutes](https://www.ura.org/media/W1siZiIsIjIwMjMvMTIvMTgvN2l2eGVjYmxib19OT1ZFTUJFUl8yMDIzX1VSQV9SRUdVTEFSX0JPQVJEX01FRVRJTkdfTUlOVVRFU19GSU5BTC5wZGYiXV0/NOVEMBER%202023%20URA%20REGULAR%20BOARD%20MEETING%20MINUTES%20%20-%20FINAL.pdf) list 1623 and 1701 Lanark as rehab units within a 15 home CLT package across six neighborhoods, using New Markets Tax Credit equity, a construction loan, philanthropy, and a URA grant. That package's $7.99 million total is **not** a Lanark per home cost. The [City of Bridges Lanark project page](https://cityofbridgesclt.org/our-properties/lanark1/) now labels the effort rehab and new construction and gives 2026/2027 expected completion, while older dates remain elsewhere on the same page. Do not treat that page as evidence of completed work or a stable timeline.

**Experiment:** run the same screening template twice, once for 21-C-213 as a historic built-form comparison, and once for a specific proposed Lanark project parcel only after its parcel ID and design are confirmed. Ask whether the brief can state today's zoning district, current use standards, location-based reviews, owner route, and unanswered conditions for **an attached affordable home** without declaring legal approval. Add a finance section that lists known sources from the dated URA record and labels all current costs, sales proceeds, subsidy commitment, and gap unknown. This reveals whether a clear zoning answer without project economics would be useful.

## Case 2: 3525 Beechwood Boulevard, Greenfield, fourplex comparison

**Why it is useful:** a small four unit form tests the difference between an existing building and a hypothetical new fourplex on the same lot. Billings and John Akey's [2024 case essay](https://www.prohousingpgh.org/blog/you-cant-build-that-here-3525-beechwood-blvd) identifies parcel 88-B-44, reports four existing apartments, about 5,007 sq ft of land, and argues a newly built fourplex would conflict with the then applicable R2-M use, unit density, setback, and parking standards. This is the authors' dated analysis. The existing use does **not** imply a new project can be built or that a variance is guaranteed. No finance model or proposed developer decision is documented there.

**Experiment:** compare two explicit scenarios: preserve/rehab the existing four units versus demolish and construct a new fourplex. Output the current source-dated zoning/use questions for both, plus unknown title, building condition, site work, rent, cost, and subsidy. Ask an SME whether this distinction changes their first paid diligence decision. Do not claim the new fourplex is currently prohibited solely from the 2024 article.

## Current-rule and physical-risk guardrails

The City's [housing code update page](https://engage.pittsburghpa.gov/implementing-housing-needs-assessment) says minimum lot size amendments were approved in May 2025, after both Billings case articles. City [public notices as updated 2026-09-15](https://www.pittsburghpa.gov/Business-Development/City-Planning/Public-Notices) show an accessory dwelling, affordable housing bonus, and parking reform bill going to a 2026-09-23 City Council public hearing. A hearing and proposal are not enactment. Before calculating setbacks, density, parking, or allowed uses, check the effective text and map for the specific parcel as of the demonstration date; capture the ordinance version. The Billings numbers cannot simply be carried into a 2026 rule engine.

City [Planning Applications and Processes](https://www.pittsburghpa.gov/Business-Development/City-Planning/Zoning/Planning-Applications-and-Processes) states review level varies with project scope and location, and specifically calls out H (Hillside) district single family cases for Site Plan Review. That supports a flag, not an automatic geotechnical verdict. The SME's topography, undermining, and flood concerns should become three separate items with data source and human check. For each parcel, record whether an official mapped layer gives a flag, then mark site specific engineering, mine risk, and flood compliance conclusions unknown until reviewed. This bounded research did not verify parcel-level values for either case.

## Recommended next concrete experiment

Create two static one-page **site plus proposal** briefs, no scoring engine yet: Lanark attached affordable home and Beechwood preserve versus rebuild fourplex. In each, separate (1) verified parcel and dated current code facts, (2) what the proposal might trigger, (3) unresolved physical risks, and (4) a very small finance box with only known or user-supplied values. Have the SME annotate each brief: which assertion is wrong, which missing fact would change the decision, who reviews it, and what expense occurs first. This tests whether "what is allowed" and "what pencils" truly belong in the same first screen. A single legacy building is a validation case, not a candidate development site or proof of current feasibility.
