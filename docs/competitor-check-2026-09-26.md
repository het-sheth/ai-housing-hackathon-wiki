## Implication across four plausible roles

The existing sources serve different jobs, so avoid a premature single-persona product claim. A nonprofit/CLT acquisition screener most needs a defensible next action and ownership/program path; a small builder or architect may value the Site Plan Creator/TestFit concept and envelope outputs; public planning/agency staff already have local record/map workflows; and a board/funder reviewer may need a sourced, legible rationale plus costs/affordability readiness. These are candidate role distinctions, not validated personas. The product gap, if any, is likely a shared decision handoff, not merely a map or PDF.

## Strongest threat to the idea

The strongest threat is not a single vendor. It is the combination of the City's and County's free parcel/zoning/permit tools with the Land Bank's already-published local due-diligence guide. For a one-parcel factual brief, an informed staffer can already assemble much of the record without buying software. For a proposed physical concept, Site Plan Creator and TestFit already document property/zoning/site-plan outputs, with TestFit also documenting early financial evaluation. Thus “aggregation into a report” alone is weak differentiation.

A potentially distinct job is **concept-conditioned, source-linked decision support for one chosen affordable-housing pathway**: given a parcel and a simple, explicit housing concept, explain which gates are supported, which are unresolved, whether the nonprofit's actual acquisition/funding path appears plausible, and what specific paid or authoritative check should happen next. This is a hypothesis, not proven whitespace: no reviewed competitor docs establish that they do this for Pittsburgh nonprofit/CLT users, and none establishes that users want or trust it. That must be tested separately with all four candidate roles before claiming a market gap.

## Evidence boundaries

- No comparative hands-on trial was done; no conclusion about relative accuracy, usability or completeness is warranted.
- Pittsburgh coverage is confirmed for the official City/County sources and PLB guide. It is **unverified at parcel/rule level** for Site Plan Creator, Zoneomics, TestFit and Deepblocks based on the primary pages reviewed.
- No conclusion that a competitor “cannot” support nonprofit workflows is justified from feature pages. The specific affordable-housing, CLT ownership, local source provenance, low-volume pricing and decision-gate questions remain open.

## Practical SME question

“Walk me through the most recent affordable-home site you considered. Before paying for the next study, what exact pursue/stop decision did you need to make, what evidence was missing, and which City/County/Land Bank or commercial tools did you already use? Would a source-linked brief for that site and your actual home concept have changed the decision or the next dollar spent? If this differs for your staff, design, agency and board/funder roles, who sees which artifact?”
