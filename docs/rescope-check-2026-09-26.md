## Pittsburgh coverage and boundaries

**Pittsburgh coverage is not verified and available official pages conflict on geographic reach.** Rescope's [Rezone product page](https://www.rescope.co/products/rezone/) says “5 jurisdictions covered today” and enumerates Los Angeles City/County, San Diego City/County and San Jose, with expansion across California. Another indexed version of that same official page says “3,000+ jurisdictions” and “any address in the US.” The [documentation](https://www.rescope.co/resources/documentation/) is more cautious: not all jurisdictions are supported and unsupported addresses return “Coverage Unavailable.” These inconsistencies mean the national marketing language is not evidence that a Pittsburgh parcel will work. Only an address/APN test within an account, if the team chooses to do so, can resolve coverage.

Rescope says the zoning report uses official codes, assessor parcel data and municipal GIS. The reviewed public docs do not name Pittsburgh source layers, show a Pittsburgh report, or substantiate rule-by-rule Pittsburgh accuracy. Rescope's ADU/builder page advertises slope, easements, utilities and permits; the pages reviewed do not document their underlying sources or methodology for Pittsburgh, and they do not establish utility capacity. Do not represent those as verified local facts.

No report PDF or interactive report output was publicly available to inspect. The homepage embeds a **“Compliance Sample”** with Zone C-2, 94.7%, 12.5 ft setback, FAR 0.85, three violations and 35 ft height, plus sample parcel-view imagery. Clicking the official image links failed with cache-miss errors, so the sample's provenance links and full output could not be visually checked. The documentation page, rather than a sample artifact, provides the clearest claimed output specification.

No published pricing was found. Rescope says a first analysis is free without a credit card, but access requires creating an account and verifying an email. The homepage “Request a feasibility report” form asks for name, email, company and location; demo links require scheduling. No signup or contact was attempted.

## What this means for our concept

Rescope is a direct and substantial competitor to the **parcel-plus-concept zoning/ease brief**: it explicitly markets parcel-resolved constraints, source-cited zoning, go/no-go, dimensional limits and use categories; developer materials add site pipeline and nearby approvals. A generic address-in/report-out feasibility card, PDF, or zoning “ease score” is not a defensible distinction by itself.

There is a narrower, unproven differentiation hypothesis: a screen tailored to a nonprofit's actual affordable-homeownership/rental program and acquisition path, showing what is known, what remains unknown, and the next decision or paid diligence step. Rescope's public materials reviewed do not document nonprofit affordability restrictions, URA/PHFA program eligibility, CLT ground-lease/homebuyer fit, subsidy sources/uses, or an affordable-housing operating pro forma. This is strictly “not documented in pages reviewed,” not proof Rescope lacks it. Similarly, its “investment memos” and “market comparables” could overlap more than public descriptions reveal.

A local product should not imply engineering, title, utility-capacity, environmental or financial determination from GIS. Better test a **concept-conditioned next-action workflow** using the local official data stack, and see if that changes an actual proceed/stop/diligence decision. Consider all four candidate roles (nonprofit/CLT screener, designer/builder, public/agency reviewer, board/funder): Rescope's public customer pages speak mainly to builders, consultancies, architects and developers, while the proposed funding-path value is most likely relevant to nonprofit staff and their approvers. Actual users and their paid decision gate remain unvalidated.

## Strongest next product experiment

Run one **blind side-by-side decision test** on a real but non-sensitive Pittsburgh infill parcel and a defined affordable-home concept: participants from the four candidate roles first use only the current City/County/Land Bank resources, then review a mock one-page brief that adds program/financing-path fit, explicit unknowns and next actions. Ask each to commit to proceed, stop or pay for which next check, and identify the cited fact that changed their choice. Separately, if Rescope coverage can be checked without a sales/demo request, compare its parcel-zoning result as another input; otherwise mark that competitor benchmark unavailable. The test should decide whether the distinctive nonprofit decision support adds value beyond existing free tools and Rescope's documented zoning job, rather than evaluate whether people like a report format.
