## Product implication

**Shared core:** parcel identity and jurisdiction; public ownership and acquisition route; City zoning/review facts; public condition flags; proposed tenure, use and unit count; evidence link and retrieval date for every finding; unknowns and the responsible next human. Separate observed data from inferred review path. County portal explicitly says its owner/sale history is incomplete and a deed search is needed for a complete history [5].

**Primary weekend workflow to test:** user enters one Pittsburgh parcel **and a proposed project** (for example, rehab one house for sale or build three rental units), then receives a short, source linked screening brief for the decision to investigate further. Show the brief to any of the four personas and ask which real decision it would change. Do not build four dashboards. Do not default the wording to nonprofits before this test.

**Score distinction:** a parcel has intrinsic observable attributes such as dimensions, zoning district, mapped overlays, and owner. Development ease depends on a *proposed use and design*, acquisition path, funding, and organizational objective. A site that is easy for a market rate house can be hard for a small affordable rental project. The weekend output should show factors and unresolved questions, not assert a definitive site grade. If a single score is required for demonstration, label it a transparent summary of checked factors and keep mission fit separate.

**Highest remaining unknown:** which persona has a frequent, consequential decision made from scattered public facts and a reusable artifact. No official source in this check establishes that the nonprofit's early parcel screen is the highest pain point, that developers need a new screen, that planners would accept an external tool, or that policy analysts decide one parcel at a time.

## One organizer channel question

**Team / track:** Het and Rushi, Track 1 Development Ease Navigator.

**What we are building:** A short, source linked screen for one Pittsburgh site and a proposed housing project, showing public facts and what still needs a person to check.

**Our question:** Among planners, small developers, housing nonprofits, and policy analysts, who most often has to make an early decision from scattered site information? For one recent real case, what decision did they make, what document did they use, and what missing fact led to the first paid check or review?

**What we currently believe:** The useful result is a brief that helps decide the next investigation. We have not validated which role needs it most, or whether a site brief fits their current work.
