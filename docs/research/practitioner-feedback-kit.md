# Practitioner feedback kit

Contribution: Rushi Patel's September 26, 2026 discussion; consolidated September 27. Proposed protocol. No outreach or usability session was performed as part of this contribution.

## Whom to ask

Start with a development/project manager who coordinates design, zoning and financing for small housing projects. Use an architect or planning reviewer for specific rule/input questions. An initial SME reaction is concept feedback; it does not establish delivery-side adoption or validate the interface.

Find official public organizational contact routes. Record why each role fits the project family and decision stage. Do not assume availability or collect private contacts.

## Outreach draft

We are building a small Pittsburgh housing hackathon prototype that compares two proposals on one property and explains changed checks, unknowns and next actions. Could you spend 15 minutes critiquing one sample brief? We want to understand what is missing or misleading and whether it would change a real project decision. We can also send the brief with three questions if that is easier.

This is a draft for the team to send, not authorization for automated outreach.

## Fifteen-minute interview

1. First 5 minutes: Tell us about the last property you screened. What decision were you making? What did you know, and what was hardest to obtain? Who approved spending on the next step?
2. Next 5 minutes: What document or tool did you use? Who received it? Did a finding change the proposed scope? What happened next? Ask for a public or appropriately anonymized example, not confidential client records.
3. Final 5 minutes: Show the sample brief. Ask what is incorrect, missing or already obvious. Ask what the participant would do next and what evidence they would need first.

Avoid asking whether an AI tool would save time. Record actual behavior separately from predictions.

## Ten-minute brief exercise

Give the participant the same property and two explicitly labeled proposals. Ask them to identify the housing objective, changed checks, persistent unknowns and next action without explanation from the facilitator. Then ask them to change one proposal input and explain what they expect to change.

| Signal | Observation to record |
|---|---|
| Comprehension | Can they distinguish a source fact from an assumption? |
| Actionability | Can they name a document, responsible role and reason for the next step? |
| False certainty | Do they read a conditional finding as approval or an unchecked item as clear? |
| Comparison value | Does the comparison affect a real action or merely repeat what they already knew? |
| Input burden | Can they supply the fields at this stage without doing the entire professional review first? |

Do not invent numerical success thresholds from a tiny sample. Record errors and concrete changes to test.

## Asynchronous fallback

Attach one brief and ask:

1. Which statement or missing fact could make this comparison misleading?
2. Who would use this, and at what point in an actual project?
3. What would they do differently after reading it?

## Notes template

Role/project type; date; permission to retain/share; actual recent decision; existing artifact; observed confusion; missing input; proposed next action; researcher interpretation; design change; unresolved question.

Keep identifiable participant notes private. Public research should use only authorized, suitably anonymized findings. A model-generated persona is not a participant. Without interviews, compare public artifacts and label remaining adoption questions unknown.
