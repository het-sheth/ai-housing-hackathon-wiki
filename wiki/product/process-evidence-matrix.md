# Pittsburgh process matrix draft: one new detached dwelling

Checked September 27, 2026. Research draft for a hypothetical new detached single-family dwelling of three stories or less within confirmed Pittsburgh city jurisdiction. No specific parcel, site plan, lawful baseline, zoning classification, hazard review, right-of-way scope or utility design has been supplied. This matrix is a source-backed diligence route, not a permit determination or an assessed Development Ease factor. It does not apply by default to other Allegheny County municipalities, attached homes, taller structures, multiple dwellings or mixed uses.

## Current entry route and source conflict

The City's Building & Development Application page (S1), last updated September 2, 2026, says BDA replaces the separate Zoning Development Review (ZDR) and building permit applications for scopes that include a new structure. ZDR reviews are incorporated in BDA. PLI, City Planning (DCP) and Mobility and Infrastructure (DOMI) review as relevant; staff identify additional permits, and a DOMI reviewer creates a sub-permit when needed. The OneStopPGH center (S2), last updated September 23, 2026, describes BDA as the construction application and separate linked trade sub-permits as applicable. A new detached house fitting the City's residential definition is a new-construction work type. City permit definitions (S3) distinguish residential single-family structures from commercial or mixed-use structures.

The DCP Planning Application and Process page (S4), last updated April 15, 2026, still says every development review project requires a separate ZDR and describes a ROZA before a PLI permit. The 2021 PLI Standard Permit Application Requirements guideline (S5) likewise references concurrent ZDR. Those instructions conflict with the later BDA page on how to file. Working interpretation: use BDA as the current intake path, while preserving DCP's described review topics as possible internal review steps. Confirm the actual OneStopPGH application path, ROZA document behavior and any transitional exceptions with PLI/DCP before encoding a deterministic route. No outreach has been made.

For a genuinely vacant lot, document the vacant baseline from current condition and relevant lot/City records; a CO is not a prerequisite. For an existing structure or conflicting use claims, review PLI records and resolve discrepancies. Confirm any address request with DCP. A County class or permit history alone does not settle lawful use. |
| 2. BDA intake and documents | OneStopPGH determines documents after work scope, address and co-applicants are entered. The City requires an application fee and accepts submission through OneStopPGH or the counter. The 2019 DCP checklist (S7) lists a professionally stamped site plan for new construction, and the 2021 PLI guideline (S5) lists construction drawings and possible engineering reports. Their submission instructions predate BDA. | Not verified against current portal. Generate the BDA document checklist for this exact scope and have the design professional confirm whether a stamped survey/site plan, building drawings, geotechnical or structural report is required. Do not present the older checklist as a current universal requirement. |
| 3. Zoning review inside BDA | DCP describes Basic Zoning Review for most single-family new construction in residential districts, Site Plan Review for houses in P/H districts and some neighborhood business districts, and possible Planning Commission review in named districts or for development affecting a natural steep slope. The later BDA page changes filing, not necessarily the underlying review level. DCP planning (S4), BDA (S1) | DCP zoning staff; Planning Commission only when applicable | Current zoning district and overlays for the full site; proposed use, lot dimensions, setbacks, height, coverage, parking/access, site plan and any exceptions or existing lawful-use evidence. | Review level and compliance unknown. Ask DCP to identify review tier, current code sections, any variance/special exception, notice or hearing, and whether a ROZA is produced within BDA. A mapped district is not a use or dimensional decision. |
| 4. Building code review | PLI reviews submitted drawings for code compliance; the City lists application review after submission and before issuance. Its published residential BDA initial review service level is 15 business days, with eight business days for a revision, measured from a completed accepted application and not a completion promise. PLI review (S8), permit process (S6) | PLI plan review and Building Code Official | Current construction plans, code basis, design professional input where required, responses to plan review comments and conditions. | Unassessed. Obtain current PLI residential review checklist and submit only after scope and required documents are confirmed. No review outcome can be inferred from intake fields. |
| 5. Conditional parallel reviews | New electrical/mechanical systems have separate trade permits linked to the BDA. Plumbing permits belong to Allegheny County Health Department. BDA review includes DOMI when relevant; a curb cut, sidewalk or right-of-way work may trigger DOMI sub-permit. Historic-district exterior new construction goes through HRC large-project review; floodplain, grading/stormwater, steep slope, landslide, undermining or other site conditions may require additional review and engineering. OneStopPGH (S2), other agency coordination (S9), historic review (S10), PLI guideline (S5) | PLI, DCP, DOMI, HRC, County Health Department and other providers according to trigger | Trade design, access/curb cut and ROW plan, historic designation, mapped and field hazard evidence, disturbance area, flood panel, drainage and geotechnical facts. | Each trigger remains unknown for the hypothetical site. Screen the full parcel and planned work, then obtain authority-specific applicability and document requirements. Map intersections or proximity alone cannot clear or require a permit. |
| 6. Issuance and licensed holder | PLI issues after its conditions and fees are met. Its issuance page (S11) names a PLI general contractor license for residential new construction, with listed exemptions. That 2025 page still refers to separately issued ZDR, so confirm BDA-era mechanics. The City says work covered by a permit begins after issuance. | PLI and permit holder | Notice of Construction Plan Approval, cleared conditions, fee receipt, proper license or documented exemption, and issued permits/sub-permits. | None obtained. Confirm current issuance conditions and license status with PLI; do not treat application submission or plan review as permission to build. |
| 7. Inspections, closeout and occupancy | The City requires passed inspections, completed sub-permits and closeout documents for completion. OneStopPGH says single-family dwellings generally do not require a Certificate of Occupancy; the completion page says a CO issues only if the building permit includes one, otherwise the final inspection summary documents completion. OneStopPGH (S2), Permit completion (S12) | PLI inspectors and Building Code Official | Permit-specific inspection schedule, passed final inspections, trade closeout, conditions and any CO requirement shown on the issued permit. | Future dependency. Track the issued permit and final inspection summary. Ask PLI about a CO if the actual permit specifies one or records conflict; do not assume every detached house receives a CO. |

## Open questions to resolve before a process factor can be assessed

1. Does the confirmed parcel lie wholly in Pittsburgh, and what is its legal baseline, address and exact proposed building/site scope?
2. For this application in the current OneStopPGH portal, is BDA the only parent application, and how are the DCP review outcome and ROZA recorded? The September BDA page and older DCP/PLI instructions conflict on separate ZDR filing.
3. What current zoning review tier, code provisions, exceptions, dimensional standards and site plan/survey requirements apply to the full proposal? Does any natural slope, H/P district, designated historic area or other overlay trigger a hearing or additional review?
4. Which current portal documents and qualified professional stamps are required? The older ZDR checklist and permit forms are useful leads but cannot substitute for a current BDA checklist.
5. Do proposed trades, grading, drainage, floodplain work, curb cut or ROW work trigger separate city, County or provider approvals, and what dependencies appear on the BDA record?
6. What conditions, license, inspection, closeout and occupancy documentation would PLI require for this issued permit? No application or approval exists in this research draft.
