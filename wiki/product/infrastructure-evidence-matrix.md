# Pittsburgh one-home infrastructure evidence matrix

Research draft checked September 27, 2026. Scope: one proposed new detached dwelling on a parcel confirmed to be inside Pittsburgh. This is a proposed future evidence gate, not an implemented rule or parcel finding. No particular parcel, address, utility account, site plan, fixture schedule or provider letter has been supplied. Every row remains **unassessed**. A service-area map, nearby line, completed intake field or fetched record does not establish service availability, capacity, a buildable connection or permission. The project lead must resolve each row for the exact parcel and proposed loads before treating the infrastructure rubric factor as assessed. No numeric score or range follows from this matrix.

## Evidence gate for the infrastructure factor

Keep this factor **unassessed** until the exact parcel and proposal have documented provider identity, water and sanitary availability, capacity or an explicit provider condition, workable connection routes, applicable permits, electric service response, and site access/drainage review. Gas is conditional on the chosen energy design. Record each source date and outstanding conflict. A letter stating that a line exists is not a capacity finding unless the issuing provider explicitly says so. A permit route or completed form is not a feasibility determination.

## Current source tension to resolve

Pittsburgh Water's residential permit description [S4] says single-family new taps can proceed without tap-in drawings, while its tap-in review page [S3] broadly says new taps need engineer-stamped plans. The site-specific permit classification and any exceptions must be confirmed with Pittsburgh Water before encoding a requirement for this one-home proposal.

The official indexed text for Title 13 Section 1303.01 [S10] was available to this research check on September 27, 2026, but direct retrieval returned HTTP 403. The complete current section text, amendment status and effective version were therefore not independently retrieved. Recheck the enacted text and any later amendment through an accessible official record before translating stormwater thresholds into application logic.

## Deterministic cases before practitioner review

1. Parcel provider unknown, even with a nearby line: keep provider, availability and capacity unresolved; request address-specific provider confirmation.
2. Provider confirms its territory but gives no design-capacity response: retain capacity and connection as unknown; do not infer a feasible tap.
3. One-home residential permit route is confirmed: record its actual drawing requirements. A development-route letter is required only if the provider classifies this proposal on that route.
4. Proposed all-electric home: mark gas not applicable and retain electric load, service point and capacity as separate unresolved questions.
5. Stormwater threshold inputs or lawful outfall route missing: retain applicability and drainage feasibility as unknown, even if a map shows no flood flag.
6. Utility route crosses a road or third-party land: retain access and connection as unknown until the road owner or easement authority responds.

A Pittsburgh Water permitting reviewer, local sewer authority, serving electric utility and civil/design practitioner should review these gates against one real, consented parcel and proposal before implementation. No outreach is authorized by this draft.
