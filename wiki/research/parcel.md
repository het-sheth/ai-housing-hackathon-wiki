# What is a parcel?

A parcel is a defined piece of land recorded by the county, usually with an identifier such as a parcel ID or block-and-lot number. It may contain no buildings, one building, or multiple buildings. A development can involve one or several parcels.

The catalog distinguishes parcel boundaries (map polygons) from assessment records (attributes such as recorded use, assessed values and building characteristics). A parcel ID is the likely link between them. An address is a location label and can be less reliable for joining records. A census tract is a statistical geographic area used for many demographic measures, not a parcel.

Example for understanding, not a real property: a vacant lot with ID A and neighboring lot B are two parcels. A proposed apartment project could require both. A tract-level income estimate describes the larger area; it does not reveal the income of the household at parcel A.

Parcel boundaries and assessment tables can be updated on different schedules. Missing data does not prove a site has no constraints. Property ownership does not establish availability for purchase.
