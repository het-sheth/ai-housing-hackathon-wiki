# Deciphering Rushi's research

## Plain-language interpretation

Rushi's report asks: before spending serious money developing a site, can someone find out what might stop the project, what evidence supports that conclusion, and what they should do next?

The report's proposed product is a Parcel-to-Keys Evidence Graph. Its scope is much larger than a weekend: title, zoning, physical conditions, infrastructure, review process, delay and financing. Nodes represent requirements or facts; dependencies explain what must happen before something else. The report does not require a graph database to express this idea.

## What the report actually proposes

1. Input: parcel plus proposed housing concept.
2. Evidence: source, date, status, uncertainty, responsible party and dependencies.
3. Output: explained barriers and next actions; a score is secondary to the evidence.
4. Distinguish observed facts, derived findings, assumptions, unknowns, conflicts and value judgments.
5. Validate with real practitioners and three contrasting project archetypes (PDF pp. 25-32; Markdown lines 842-1201).

The research distinguishes shortages of housing in the right location, affordability, buildable land, utility capacity and homes actually available. A zoning permission alone does not establish that development is physically or financially feasible. The report discusses Pittsburgh's old buildings, slopes, fragmented ownership and infrastructure as relevant complications; these are research claims to validate when used in a product.

## Proposed weekend interpretation, not yet approved

A focused feasibility screener for a small developer or housing nonprofit considering one housing type on a few real Pittsburgh parcels. Show a parcel card, verified zoning reference, one or two spatial constraint checks, evidence links, unknowns and next questions for a planner. If the team keeps the brief's requested score, expose component rules and data coverage rather than claiming a validated probability of approval.

Candidate exclusions: automated title clearance, utility-capacity certification, approval probability, citywide code interpretation, full pro forma and predicted permit delays. These need evidence or access not established by the supplied materials.

A useful demo could contrast a relatively straightforward parcel and one with a documented constraint, showing what changes and why. Real parcel selection and housing type remain open.

## Research limitations

The PDF is 36 pages, including references; the 1,201-line Markdown covers substantially the same report but contains opaque citation markers rather than direct source URLs. Do not count them as independent corroboration. The report's delay percentages are explicitly invented illustrations. Financial examples are scenarios, not observed project results. Current legal, market and policy claims require checking against the cited sources before use.
