# The three tracks

| Track | Who and decision | Brief's expected outcome | Data in the organizer catalog |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1: Development Feasibility & Pro Forma Navigator | Planners, small developers, nonprofits comparing sites | Explainable Development Ease Score; development barriers and review flags with sources | Parcels, assessments, zoning rules and polygons, permits, hazards, market context |
| 2: Housing Production, Rents & Household Flow Observatory | Policymakers, researchers, residents examining housing changes | Interactive observatory connecting production, affordability and demand with visible quality/privacy limits | ACS, HUD CHAS, permits, sales, market aggregates, employment and mobility |
| 3: Housing Typology, Equity & Climate Matchmaker | Planners, CDCs and developers comparing housing forms | At least two scenarios for a real place, adjustable value weights, transparent equity/climate tradeoffs | CHAS, demographics, land use, transit, flood/slope/land cover, modeled energy |

The packet abbreviates Track 1's name to Development Feasibility Navigator. Although its brief title includes Pro Forma, neither that brief's stated outputs nor the packet explicitly requires a detailed financial model.

Shared expectations: working prototype, at least one Pittsburgh/Allegheny County use case, real regional data where possible, assumptions and uncertainty, and identifiable human review points. Prototype examples in the briefs are possibilities, not a requirement to implement every example. Track 2 leaves the specific AI role open. Track 3 requires distinguishing data from value judgments.

## Scope interpretation for a two-person weekend

These are planning assessments, not organizer requirements: Track 1 can focus on a few supported parcels and one housing concept; Track 2 on one geographic level and question; Track 3 on two housing types in one neighborhood. Rushi's report provides the most developed direction for Track 1, but that is not a team selection.
